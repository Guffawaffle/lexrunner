attestations/# Employment and IP Separation Statement

**Author:** Joseph Gustavson
**Date:** 2025-11-26

This statement clarifies the relationship between my personal work on the "Lex" family of projects and any software or systems owned or operated by my employer.

## 1. Personal project status

Lex, LexRunner, LexSona, and any related tooling are personal projects that I work on outside of my employment.

They are designed, implemented, and maintained under my own direction and are not part of my employer's product roadmap or internal tooling.

## 2. No use of Lex in employer systems

Lex and related projects:

- Have **never been installed, deployed, or integrated** into any production, staging, QA, or development environments owned or operated by my employer.
- Are **not** used in any of my employer's commercial products or internal systems.
- Are developed and tested on infrastructure and repositories that are separate from my employer's codebases.

## 3. No reuse of employer code or trade secrets

The Lex codebase does **not** contain:

- Source code copied from my employer's repositories.
- Proprietary configurations, schemas, algorithms, or implementation details taken from my employer.
- Trade secrets or confidential business logic belonging to my employer.

My professional experience as a software engineer informs my general design taste and architectural decisions, but I do not transplant non-public employer code or designs into Lex.

## 4. Use of AI tools

I use AI tools (including ChatGPT and other coding assistants) both:

- For personal projects like Lex, and
- For reasoning about work-related problems where this is allowed by company policy.

When discussing work topics with AI tools, I follow the relevant company guidelines (for example, avoiding PHI, credentials, and other strictly confidential data).

Ideas, patterns, or abstractions that emerge from those conversations are applied to Lex only in a **general, non-employer-specific** way and **without copying** any employer-owned code or proprietary implementation.

## 5. IP ownership and conflict of interest

Lex and related projects are developed and released under open-source and/or personal licensing that is separate from my employer's products and codebases.

To the best of my knowledge:

- Lex does not reuse employer-owned code or confidential information.
- Lex does not create a conflict of interest with my current role, because it is not used inside my employer's systems and does not compete with any product I am responsible for at work.
